## 1. The Landscape of Semantic Correspondence

> **Scientific Posture Statement:** Within the threat model defined in this work, we have not identified an existing semantic framework that directly expresses or enforces this specific correspondence relation ($P_3$) under an operational execution model consuming dynamic operational artifacts.

## 2. Evaluating the Frontiers of Correspondence

We analyze how close the nearest adjacent paradigms come to expressing our target relation, and establish the precise boundary where they decouple.

### 2.1 Authorization Logics & Proof-Carrying Authorization (PCA)
*   **Paradigms Evaluated:** "Says" Calculus (Abadi et al.), Binder, SecPAL, DKAL, Nexus Authorization Logic.
*   **What They Prove:** They verify statement derivation and principal delegation logic using deductive machinery:
    $$\Gamma \vdash K \text{ says } \phi$$
    Establishing that an assertion or request is legitimately signed and authorized by a principal.
*   **The Semantic Disconnect:** Authorization logics reason exclusively about the possession of keys and the delegation of static policy assertions. They decide whether a principal is allowed to call an operation ($e$). They completely lack the semantic primitives to evaluate the dynamic operational artifact ($\mathcal{A}$) or execution planning trace constructed during evaluation. They cannot reason about whether a dynamically derived execution graph generated by an interpretation pass remains a valid semantic consequence of $\Lambda$ when the target parameters are not statically known at the time of delegation.

### 2.2 Proof-Producing Computation
*   **Paradigms Evaluated:** Certified abstract interpreters, proof-producing SMT/SAT solvers, logical frameworks (LF, Twelf), and Coq/Lean proof terms.
*   **What They Prove:** They generate a certificate ($\pi$) proving that a computation or solution path satisfies a tight internal invariant:
    $$\text{CheckCert}(e, \pi) \equiv \text{Valid}$$
*   **The Semantic Disconnect:** These certifications are locked in isolated computational silos. They prove execution correctness or algebraic properties. However, their certificates lack any semantic features to link back to a delegation boundary ($\Lambda$) representing upstream human intent or coarse authority boundary rights under an adversarial model. The certificate proves that the computation was correct according to local language rules, but not that it respected a delegated obligation.

## 3. The Target Relationship: Semantic Consequence Preservation

Our target relation ($P_3$) bridges authorization constraints and operational execution:

$$\Sigma \models \text{Preserves}(\Lambda, e)$$

We require that for every terminal execution effect $e$, the operational evaluation derivation $\Sigma; \Lambda \vdash I \Longrightarrow e$ guarantees that $e$ sits strictly within the semantic envelope authorized by $\Lambda$.

This matrix and survey confirm that this class of relationship is fundamentally unmapped in the literature: Program Logics assume $C$ is fixed; Authorization Logics assume execution is a black-box parameter; Proof-Producing systems verify arithmetic, execution semantics, or solver constraints without modeling delegated authority propagation.
